Executive Overview

Executive Summary

Higher education institutions host a vibrant ecosystem of student extracurricular entities comprising both Clubs (technical societies, cultural troupes, hobby circles) and Cells (statutory bodies such as the Institution Innovation Council, Entrepreneurship Development Cell, Women Development Cell, NSS, and NCC). While vital for holistic student development and mandatory for institutional accreditation (NAAC Criterion 5, NBA, and NIRF rankings), managing dozens of independent organizations through fragmented paper forms, spreadsheets, and informal chat messaging creates severe administrative inefficiencies.

The Centralized Clubs & Cells Management System introduces a unified, role-governed digital operating platform developed specifically for Kongu Engineering College (KEC). It unifies all campus organizations into a single-pane discovery directory, automates membership workflows, manages flagship event registrations with capacity quotas, conducts real-time attendance roll-calls, accelerates student On-Duty (OD) academic regularization, verifies student achievements, and aggregates one-click accreditation analytics.

Core Institutional Objective
Eliminate paper sign-ups, eradicate delays in academic On-Duty (OD) regularizations, provide transparent 4-tier role governance, and automate verifiable semester achievement metrics for faculty and administrative compliance.

Section 1

Problem Statements & Need Analysis

An in-depth empirical study of campus extracurricular management revealed five critical bottlenecks spanning students, student coordinators, faculty advisors, and institutional leadership:

1. Information Siloing & Fragmented Student Discovery

Discovery Bottleneck

Observed Friction: Recruitment notices, workshop schedules, and hackathon announcements are scattered across informal WhatsApp groups, physical department notice boards, and personal social media pages.

Impact: Students (especially 1st and 2nd year undergraduates) miss vital opportunities aligned with their skills. Technical clubs struggle to recruit candidates outside their immediate department, leading to skewed participation.

Design Thinking HMW: How might we provide a unified single-pane directory where students can explore every campus club and cell, inspect leadership rosters, and apply with 1-click admission requests?

2. Inefficient Administrative Overhead & Manual Workflows

Administrative Waste

Observed Friction: Membership applications, core executive interviews (President, Secretary, Treasurer), and candidate selections rely on physical paper forms or ad-hoc Google Forms.

Impact: Coordinators spend up to 15 hours per event collating spreadsheets. Applicant status remains untracked, notifications fail to reach students, and records are lost during annual leadership handovers.

Design Thinking HMW: How might we digitize the membership lifecycle with structured application rosters, transparent statuses (Pending, Approved, Rejected, Suspended), and role progressions?

3. Attendance Verification & Academic On-Duty (OD) Friction

Academic Risk

Observed Friction: Event attendance is logged on loose paper sheets. For students to receive institutional On-Duty (OD) attendance clearance for missed classes, physical lists must be manually countersigned by club coordinators, faculty advisors, and departmental heads.

Impact: Sign-in sheets get misplaced or delayed past monthly attendance cutoff deadlines, resulting in students receiving unexcused absences and attendance shortage condonation penalties.

Design Thinking HMW: How might we digitize live event roll-calls and provide instant verification workflows for frictionless, paperless academic On-Duty (OD) regularization?

4. Unverified Achievements & Accreditation Data Scarcity

Compliance Overhead

Observed Friction: Preparing documentation for NAAC (Criterion 5), NBA appraisals, and NIRF rankings requires weeks of retroactive searching for student competition certificates and prizes.

Impact: Many student accomplishments go unrecorded, paper certificates are easily duplicated, and faculty lack a verified digital repository to export auditable performance summaries.

Design Thinking HMW: How might we create a verified digital extracurricular portfolio where students upload certificates, faculty authenticate legitimacy, and reports export with 1 click?

5. Lack of Role Governance & Security Auditability

Security & Governance

Observed Friction: Shared spreadsheets allow anyone with the link to alter rosters, falsify attendance, or claim coordinator roles. No immutable audit trail exists to track who created or altered records.

Impact: Inability to trace unauthorized changes, lack of hierarchical accountability between faculty mentors and student leads, and non-compliance with institutional data governance standards.

Design Thinking HMW: How might we enforce strict 4-tier Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), Row-Level Security (RLS), and timestamped audit logs for all administrative actions?

Section 3

End-to-End Stakeholder Workflows

The system standardizes every operational phase into structured digital workflows, eliminating physical paperwork and providing full traceability:

Workflow 1: Membership Discovery & Admission Lifecycle

  1. Exploration: Student navigates to the Organizations portal, filters by type (Club or Cell) or category, and examines the charter, leadership, and upcoming activities.
  2. Application: The student clicks Join Organization; a record is created in memberships with initial status pending.
  3. Coordinator Review: Club Coordinators access the Memberships queue, review the student's profile, skills, and department, and select Approve or Reject.
  4. Role Designation: The coordinator assigns a specific rank (member, core_member, secretary, treasurer, vice_president, or president).
  5. Notification: The student receives an in-app notification confirming their membership and unlocked member-only privileges.
Figure 3.1: Membership Application State Flow

Workflow 2: Flagship Event Lifecycle & RSVP Reservation

  1. Proposal & Venue Booking: Coordinator submits an event specifying type (workshop, competition, seminar), date, location, registration deadline, and participant capacity.
  2. Faculty Sanction: The assigned Faculty Coordinator validates budget, venue availability, and speaker credentials before publishing the event to the campus calendar.
  3. Student RSVP: Students view event details and reserve their seat with 1 click. The database enforces capacity caps and blocks double-booking via unique constraints.

Workflow 3: Real-Time Attendance Roll-Call & Digital On-Duty (OD)

  1. Live Roll-Call: On event day, coordinators open the Attendance roster for the selected event.
  2. Marking: Each attendee is marked as Present, Absent, Late, or Excused with instant visual feedback and saved in batch.
  3. Faculty Sign-Off: The Faculty Coordinator reviews turnout percentages and signs off digitally on the verified roster.
  4. OD Regularization: The validated list is exported directly to departmental academic advisors for official class attendance regularization.

Workflow 4: Achievement Submission & Verification

  1. Submission: Student logs an achievement (prizes won in hackathons, paper presentations, certifications) and uploads certificate proof.
  2. Verification: Coordinators or Faculty authenticate the credential, setting verified = true with a reviewer timestamp.
  3. Accreditation: The record automatically populates the student's profile and feeds into the institution's NAAC/NBA metrics.

Technical Architecture & Database Design

The system utilizes a modern decoupled Single-Page Application (SPA) architecture with a cloud-native Backend-as-a-Service (BaaS) providing real-time data streaming and database-level security:

Figure 5.1: Three-Tier Cloud Architecture

Database Entities in Schema

Table Name Primary Attributes & Datatypes Relationships & Constraints
profiles id (uuid, PK), name (text), email (text), role (text), department (text), phone (text), is_active (bool) 1:1 with auth.users; Cascades on delete; Role check constraint.
students user_id (uuid, PK), register_number (text), department (text), year (int), section (text), skills (text[]) FK to profiles.id; Unique register number.
organizations id (uuid, PK), name (text), type (club|cell), category (text), faculty_coordinator_id (uuid), status (text) FK to profiles.id for faculty & student coordinator leads.
memberships id (uuid, PK), student_id (uuid), organization_id (uuid), role (text), status (text), applied_at (tz) FKs to profiles & organizations; Unique (student_id, org_id).
events id (uuid, PK), organization_id (uuid), title (text), event_type (text), start_date (tz), maximum_participants (int) FK to organizations; Status: upcoming, ongoing, completed.
event_registrations id (uuid, PK), event_id (uuid), student_id (uuid), registration_date (tz), participation_status (text) Unique (event_id, student_id) prevents duplicate reservations.
attendance id (uuid, PK), event_id (uuid), student_id (uuid), status (present|absent|late|excused), marked_by (uuid) Official attendance record feeding On-Duty regularization.
achievements id (uuid, PK), student_id (uuid), title (text), level (college|state|national|intl), verified (bool) Certificate URL storage; Verified flag updated by faculty.
audit_logs id (uuid, PK), user_id (uuid), action (text), entity_type (text), entity_id (text), created_at (tz) Immutable audit log capturing administrative actions.

Row-Level Security (RLS) & Helper Functions

Security is implemented directly in PostgreSQL using Row-Level Security policies. Functions such as is_admin(), is_coordinator_of(org_id), and is_member_of(org_id) are evaluated within database queries, guaranteeing that unprivileged users cannot read or mutate unauthorized records even if client code is manipulated.

Design Thinking Framework Alignment

The project strictly followed the standard 5 Stages of Design Thinking taught in the KEC Design Thinking curriculum:

Stage 1: Empathize
• Conducted structured interviews with 1st-to-4th year students regarding club sign-up barriers.
• Shadowed student secretaries during event roll-calls and paper attendance collation.
• Consulted faculty advisors regarding lost On-Duty (OD) slips and last-minute NAAC data requests.
Stage 2: Define
• Synthesized insights into empathy maps and persona journey charts.
• Pinpointed the primary friction point: delays between event attendance roll-calls and departmental OD approval.
• Formulated 5 core "How Might We" statements to guide ideation.
Stage 3: Ideate
• Brainstormed a unified digital directory separating Clubs from statutory Cells.
• Designed a 4-tier delegation model (Admin → Faculty → Coordinator → Student).
• Conceived real-time attendance status toggles (Present/Absent/Late/Excused) with 1-click OD export.
Stage 4: Prototype
• Built a responsive single-page application using React 18 and Tailwind CSS.
• Created Supabase PostgreSQL schema with 11 tables and strict RLS policies.
• Integrated resilient local caching so the UI never displays blank states even offline.
Stage 5: Test & Validate
• Simulated multi-role event execution: student RSVP → live attendance roll-call → faculty OD export.
• Verified that CSV reports match the exact tabular criteria required for NAAC Criterion 5.3 and NBA criteria.
• Conducted user acceptance testing across desktop and mobile browsers, ensuring sub-100ms UI responsiveness.
Section 7

Conclusion & Future Scope

The Centralized Clubs & Cells Management System successfully resolves the systemic fragmentation and administrative friction in college extracurricular operations. By digitizing the complete lifecycle from membership applications and event ticketing to live attendance roll-calls and OD regularization, it establishes an auditable single source of truth for students, faculty mentors, and institutional leadership.

Planned Future Enhancements

  • QR Code / Geofenced Check-In: Dynamic time-limited QR codes displayed at event halls for rapid student self-attendance.
  • Direct ERP API Integration: Automated synchronization with the Kongu Engineering College academic attendance ERP to regularize On-Duty (OD) hours without manual CSV transfer.
  • Automated Digital Certificates: Automated generation of cryptographically signed, verifiable PDF participation certificates for event attendees.
